# How resale marketplaces make money (as of 23 Oct 2026)

Research for an AI resale agent (photo, then cross-list, negotiate and sell) plus an own marketplace with a buying agent. Launch market: US, EU later.

**How sources are marked.** Figures come from official help, fee or press pages that I opened, unless marked otherwise:
- **"unverified"**: I couldn't confirm the figure on an official page. Usually the official page was robots-blocked or errored, and the figure comes from trade press (Value Added Resource, Shopifreaks, TechCrunch) or from reseller blogs.
- **"(press)"**: the source is reputable trade press, not the platform itself.

**Research limits.**
- These official pages could not be fetched: Facebook help, StockX help, Poshmark support and fee pages, The RealReal commissions page, Whatnot help center and Mercari's general fees page. Rows that depend on them are flagged.
- The shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Coverage of new AI resale agents is therefore thinner than intended (see section 3).

## 2. Synthesis

### 2.1 Dominant model trends

1. **Peer-to-peer (C2C) fashion is moving to "free to sell, buyer pays a protection or platform fee."**
   - **Who has moved:** Vinted, the originator, followed by Depop UK (Mar–Apr 2024) and Depop US (Jul 2024). eBay moved in Germany (2023), the UK (Oct 2024 seller-free, Feb 2025 buyer fee), and France and Italy (Sep 2026).
   - **Typical fee:** about 5% + a fixed $0.70–$1, or a regressive tier structure like eBay's 7%/4%/2%.
   - **The pattern:** the seller-facing "0%" headline drives supply. The buyer fee is less visible because it is bundled with protection.
2. **Retrofitting a buyer fee is hard; starting with one is easy.**
   - Poshmark reversed its Oct 2024 change in about 3 weeks.
   - Mercari went fully buyer-paid in Mar 2024, then retreated in Jan 2025 to a split of 10% seller + 3.6% buyer.
   - eBay US says it has no plans to switch (Aug 2026).
   - A new entrant can launch on the buyer-fee model without the backlash incumbents face.
3. **Buyer fees are becoming dynamic and being reframed.**
   - Vinted UK now charges a 3–8% + £0.30–0.80 range.
   - Mercari has floated supply/demand pricing.
   - In Oct 2026 Vinted renamed "Buyer Protection fee" to a mandatory "Vinted fee". My inference is that this reflects consumer-law pressure on how "protection" fees are presented. Expect more scrutiny of drip pricing and fee display.
4. **Ads and boosts are the second engine, and they are getting pricier.**
   - Depop's boost fee went from 8% to 12% (Mar 2026), charged as a cost per sale.
   - eBay runs cost-per-sale, cost-per-click and offsite Promoted Listings.
   - Poshmark has Promoted Closet (cost per click), Etsy has Offsite Ads at 12–15% plus CPC ads, and Vinted sells bumps and Spotlight.
   - The RealReal is exploring third-party ads. Facebook Marketplace is almost entirely ad-funded.
   - The cost-per-sale pattern ("pay a % only if the boost drove the sale") suits AI agents well.
5. **Take rates are rising at the curated and authenticated end, and being cut for power sellers.**
   - **Rising:** Vestiaire US went from 10% to 17% (Oct 2026), and eBay added +0.35 pt (Feb 2025).
   - **Managed consignment** keeps 20–97% (ThredUp, The RealReal).
   - **Volume tiers** reward power sellers: Whatnot drops from 8% to 4.5%, GOAT varies its rate by seller rating, and eBay Store tiers lower the FVF.
6. **Subscriptions are mainly a tooling business, not a marketplace one.** Crosslisters charge about $4–$100/month. Marketplaces use subscriptions lightly: eBay Stores, Etsy Plus ($10) and Vinted Pro in the EU.
7. **Payments, shipping and fintech add margin.**
   - **Processing** is passed through, often with a markup.
   - **Payout fees:** GOAT charges 2.9% to cash out, and Mercari has an Instant Pay fee.
   - **Stored balances:** eBay keeps French and Italian sellers' funds in eBay Balance, and ThredUp pushes store credit.
   - **Shipping:** platforms run negotiated, buyer-paid flat labels and recover overweight costs (Poshmark, Oct 2026).

### 2.2 What fits a cross-platform agent plus a growing own marketplace

**Structural facts that shape the choice:**
- **Payment flow.** On third-party platforms the platform collects payment, so the agent can't take a cut at checkout. You must bill the seller separately, by card on file or from a wallet the agent manages.
- **Fee stacking.** Any seller-side % on top of eBay (13.6%), Mercari (10%) or Poshmark (20%) stacks on fees the seller already pays. On Depop or Vinted it lands on a seller paying 0%, so it is very visible.
- **Automation risk.** Automated listing and offers on Poshmark, Depop and Vinted carry terms-of-service and account-ban risk. That is a platform-dependency risk for any per-sale model.
- **Supply is the bottleneck.** The own marketplace starts with little demand. The agent's cross-listing is what seeds supply.

**Option A: success fee per agent sale.** A % of sale price, charged only when the agent completes a sale on any channel. For example 8–12%, with a minimum of about $1–2 and a cap per item.
- **Pros:**
  - Fully aligned with "photo in, money out." There is no upfront friction, and casual sellers (the core user) never see a subscription.
  - Pricing can be pitched against managed consignment, which keeps 20–97% at ThredUp and The RealReal: "you keep far more and we still do the work."
  - Scales with the value delivered.
- **Cons:**
  - It stacks on platform fees. An eBay sale costs the seller about 13.6% + 10%, which is near ThredUp territory on cheap items.
  - Collection on third-party sales needs sale detection plus off-platform billing, with chargeback and dispute exposure.
  - Revenue depends on platforms that may block automation.
  - It cuts against the market's "0% seller fee" message.

**Option B: free to sell on the own marketplace (buyer fee) plus a subscription for the cross-platform agent.**
- **How it works:**
  - **Own marketplace:** the Vinted/Depop model, with 0% seller fee and a buyer fee of about 5% + $0.70–$1 that bundles protection and support.
  - **Agent:** freemium. A few free listings a month, then about $10–30/month for unlimited cross-listing, negotiation and auto-delist. That sits within the $4–$60 crosslister range.
- **Pros:**
  - Matches where C2C fees are heading, and easy to launch with because there are no incumbent sellers to anger.
  - The subscription is predictable and needs no per-sale billing on third-party platforms.
  - The buyer fee grows automatically as own-marketplace GMV grows.
- **Cons:**
  - Casual sellers churn from or refuse monthly plans, so subscriptions mainly reach semi-pro resellers. That is a crowded market competing against $3.75–$15 tools.
  - Buyer-fee revenue is tiny until the own marketplace has liquidity.
  - Buyer fees need a strong protection product, such as authentication and returns, to be credible. They also face disclosure and drip-pricing rules in the EU and UK.

**Option C (recommended direction): hybrid that routes sales to the own marketplace.**
- **How it works:**
  1. **Free to list everywhere.**
  2. **Sold on the own marketplace:** 0% seller fee. The buyer pays a protection fee (about 5% + $0.70), and paid boosts are cost-per-sale, Depop-style.
  3. **Sold on a third-party platform by the agent:** a modest success fee (about 5–8%, with a minimum) or a power-seller subscription that waives it.
  4. **Buying agent:** earns the buyer fee on own-marketplace items and, where allowed, affiliate or referral revenue on off-platform items. Affiliate rates are unverified.
  5. **Later layers:** instant payout fee, discounted shipping labels with a small margin, and authentication fees for high-value items.
- **Pros:**
  - The pricing gap ("sell here for free, or pay X% when we sell it for you elsewhere") pushes both supply and price-sensitive buyers to the own marketplace. That is the long-term moat, and it reduces dependence on platforms that dislike automation.
  - Diversified revenue: buyer fee, ads, success fee, subscriptions and fintech.
- **Cons:**
  - It is the most complex model to explain. "Free here, paid there" must be shown transparently in the agent's "you'll net $X on each channel" view.
  - It still needs off-platform billing for third-party sales.
  - The buyer fee only matters once the own marketplace has demand. Early revenue will lean on success fees and subscriptions.
  - Steering sellers toward your own channel must not hurt their outcomes. If the agent lists on the own marketplace when eBay would net more, trust breaks.

**Practical guidance for a US launch:**
- **The headline must be "0% to sell".** Competing US apps Depop, Vinted and the eBay UK/EU model all train users to expect it.
- **Put protection and support on the buyer side.**
- **Show the net-to-seller figure per channel.** Each platform's fee stack differs a lot: Depop ~3.3% + $0.45, eBay ~13.6% + $0.40, Mercari 10%, Poshmark 20%, FB shipped 10%, Whatnot 8% + 2.9% + $0.30. Showing it is a feature in itself.
- **Charge for the agent's labor only when it produces a sale.** It is the clearest value exchange for non-pro sellers. Keep a flat subscription for power sellers, who will compare against Vendoo and SellRaze.
- **Make boosts cost-per-sale, never cost-per-click.** Cost-per-sale fits an agent that can decide when boosting pays off, as Depop does at 12%.
