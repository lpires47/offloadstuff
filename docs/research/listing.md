# Listing creation and pricing: competitive teardown

Researched 2026-10-23, for a US launch of an AI resale agent and marketplace.

**How to read this**
- **[U]** marks a claim that is unverified. It comes only from an app-store page, a vendor's or competitor's blog, a secondary outlet, or company claims I could not confirm in a primary or credible independent source.
- **VAR** is Value Added Resource (valueaddedresource.net), an independent resale-industry outlet that hands-tests these tools. It is the best independent source here.
- Several crosslister blogs (Nifty, Vendoo, Closo, OneShop, 3Dsellers, Voolist, List Perfectly) sell competing tools. I treated their claims about other products as [U] unless an official page confirmed them.
- The search budget ran out near the end. Some gaps are listed under "Open questions".

## Best in class (and what to borrow)

1. **Photo to identification: eBay Magical Listing.** It combines a knowledge graph, sold data and **guided photo capture**. Borrow: an agent that asks for the next shot ("now the tag", "the sole", "the serial"), as Poshmark's tag reading shows this works. Fall back gracefully when unsure, because eBay and Mercari errors show that one wrong ID ruins the whole listing.
2. **Multi-item / pile: Flippr** [U]. It segments one pile photo into per-item values and sell-through, then lets you confirm matches. Borrow: per-item segmentation with a keep or sell toggle on each detected object (Meta's coaster problem), duplicate detection (Homvi), and grouping by item instead of eBay's brittle fixed photos-per-draft.
3. **Attributes: eBay's confidence-gated prefill plus catalog match.** Borrow: autofill only high-confidence fields, show the rest as one-tap choices, and snap to a catalog or UPC record when one exists. Add Poshmark-style OCR of tags and care labels for size and material.
4. **Price suggestion: Depop Pricing Inspiration.** It gives a 3-point range (quick, predicted, top), each tied to speed, and **shows the sold comps**. Borrow it outright. Add Terapeak-style sell-through to produce an "expected days to sell" figure, and Poshmark's two-button "sell fast (Hot Deal) vs. max price" framing for casual sellers. Never base the price on asking prices, which is FB's weakness.
5. **Auto price drops: Poshmark Smart Sell plus eBay's deterministic schedule.** Borrow: the seller sets one floor at approval. The agent negotiates and sends offers to likers and watchers first (eBay's automatic offers), then cuts the public price on a visible schedule (eBay: every 3 days). Size each cut to clear each platform's notification threshold (Poshmark ≥10% and ≥$10; Mercari ≥5% below the historical low). Avoid Mercari's opaque cadence.
6. **Photo enhancement: Poshmark's non-destructive cover-copy background removal, under eBay's picture policy.** Borrow: edit only the background, and only on the cover. Never alter item pixels. Keep the originals and require flaw close-ups. Ban generated try-ons. Store hashes of the originals as provenance against refund fraud (the Vinted case).
7. **Authenticity: Mercari Authenticate** (required proof photos at listing above brand and price thresholds), plus **eBay and Poshmark's automatic routing**. Borrow: at listing time, detect brand × price above a threshold, prompt for authentication photos, and prefer the channel where the item gets a free guarantee (eBay sneakers ≥$100; Poshmark luxury ≥$500).
8. **"Worth selling": no one does this well on the seller side.** ThredUp is the zero-effort baseline: 60–80% accepted, about 38% of those unsold, low and opaque payout. TRR openly turns low-value items away. Borrow: for each item, show **net proceeds after fees and shipping, expected days to sell, and the seller's time cost**. Recommend sell, bundle, ThredUp-style bag, or donate. This is our clearest gap to fill.
9. **Approval UX: Nifty's "Changes card"** (agent proposes, human approves) [U], plus eBay's per-field confidence and Poshmark's "approve once, autopilot within the minimum". Borrow: one approval card per listing that shows the price range and comps, flags low-confidence fields, and sets the floor and auto-accept bounds. A second card for the final sale.
